# Positive Cases

These examples are for calibration only. They describe what worked, why it worked, and what kind of judgment future generations should reuse. Do not copy their exact composition, objects, doodles, note placement, or wording into new generations.

Why This Is Good:

- The emotional line is clear: being outdoors with a dog makes the afternoon feel lighter and more carefree.
- The notes respond to the moment instead of labeling objects. They write about ease, happiness, attention, and shared play.

Reusable Judgment:

- Good for relaxed outdoor scenes where a person and pet share one playful action.
- Use a single emotional line around companionship, fresh air, movement, and lightness.
- Mark the main subjects clearly when the background is visually busy, but keep the outline thin and photo-friendly.
- Use curved motion lines only when they help connect the subjects.
- Do not copy the exact notes, pose emphasis, dog markings, path shape, or composition.

Bad Direction To Avoid:

- Do not merely label "人", "狗", "草地", or "天气".
- Do not over-decorate the green background.

## 03 - Hiking Gear Roll Call

```text
assets/examples/positive-03-hiking-gear-roll-call.jpg
```

Current asset status: available.

Scene:

- Hiking gear is laid out before a trip, including backpack, rain shell, fleece, pants, socks, hats, gloves, toiletries, electronics, first-aid items, and small accessories.
- The frame is a professional collection: many items together form the subject.
- The intended feeling is "before departure, everything is being checked carefully".

Why This Is Good:

- The emotional line is clear: this is not a random pile of objects, but a calm pre-hike roll call before heading to the mountain.
- The image identifies many important gear categories and gives each one a short emotional function.
- The annotations create a "阅兵" feeling: each item is noticed, named, and mentally checked before departure.
- The density is high, but appropriate for a gear flat lay because the collection itself is the subject.
- The notes balance practical recognition and emotional support: warmth, safety, confidence, and readiness.
- The outlines and dotted routes help separate object groups without replacing the original photo.

Reusable Judgment:

- Good for professional collection scenes where careful item recognition matters.
- Build a compact object list first, then annotate key gear groups one by one.
- Use short functional-emotional notes: what the item does, and what feeling it gives.
- High density is acceptable when the frame is a flat lay and the user wants the collection to be read carefully.
- Conservative naming matters. If the exact item is uncertain, use a safer category name.

Bad Direction To Avoid:

- Do not treat the gear as background clutter.
- Do not write a generic travel mood while ignoring the actual equipment.
- Do not invent exact model names or technical functions.
- Do not let tiny accessories steal focus from key warmth, rain, safety, storage, and navigation items.

Scene:

- A city park or open lawn with many large kites flying over people, tents, skyline, and open sky.
- The different kites create the main visual rhythm: whale, shark, long colorful kites, and smaller flying shapes.
- The intended feeling is open, upward, playful, and positive.

Why This Is Good:

- The emotional line is clear: playing in a natural city park can lift the mood and make the day feel brighter.
- The image understands the whole park atmosphere, not just the individual kite objects.
- Different kites are used as emotional carriers. They help express upward movement, imagination, and lightness.
- The notes connect sky, wind, city edge, people sitting, and flying objects into one positive scene.
- The annotation layer feels airy and does not overfill the large sky area.
- The result gives the viewer an optimistic feeling: worries can be placed farther away, and the mood can rise with the wind.

Reusable Judgment:

- Good for park, outdoor leisure, kite, festival, and open-sky scenes.
- Use the whole-scene emotion first, then connect a few distinctive objects to that emotion.
- Let different flying objects carry slightly different notes while still supporting one upward mood.
- Keep large sky areas breathable; do not cover them with too much text.
- Use curved flight lines and light doodles only when they strengthen movement and optimism.

Bad Direction To Avoid:

- Do not only label kite types or animals.
- Do not ignore the human scale and park atmosphere.
- Do not make every kite a separate joke.
- Do not overfill the sky with dense handwriting.
